## Overview

Mental health is a state of well-being enabling individuals to cope with life stresses, realize their abilities, learn, work effectively, and contribute to their community. It holds intrinsic value and is integral to overall health.

Factors influencing mental health range from individual and family dynamics to community and structural conditions. While many demonstrate resilience, adverse circumstances—such as poverty, violence, disability, and inequality—increase the risk of mental health conditions.

Despite cost-effective treatments for many conditions, global health systems remain under-resourced, with wide treatment gaps, variable care quality, and pervasive stigma, discrimination, and human rights violations.

## Impact

Mental health conditions encompass disorders, psychosocial disabilities, and other states causing distress, functional impairment, or self-harm risk.

* **Global prevalence (2019):** 970 million people lived with a mental disorder, notably anxiety and depression.
* **Disability:** Mental disorders account for 1 in 6 years lived with disability worldwide.
* **Life expectancy:** Individuals with severe conditions die 10–20 years earlier than average.
* **Suicide & rights:** Mental health conditions raise suicide risk and the likelihood of human rights violations.
* **Economic burden:** Productivity losses exceed direct care costs.

## WHO Response

WHO Member States are advancing the **Comprehensive Mental Health Action Plan 2013–2030**, focusing on:

1. Strengthening governance and leadership.
2. Expanding community-based services.
3. Implementing promotion and prevention strategies.
4. Enhancing information systems, evidence, and research.

Key WHO initiatives include:

* **World Mental Health Report (2022):** Calls for accelerated action on promotion, prevention, and service networks.
* **Special Initiative for Mental Health (2019–2023):** Aims to extend quality, affordable care to 100 million more people in 12 countries.
* **mhGAP:** Scales up services for mental, neurological, and substance use disorders, especially in low- and middle-income settings.
* **QualityRights:** Provides training and guidance for rights-based, recovery-oriented care.
